## The sixth case carries three actor lines through live learning in one run

**Case:** Test Case 6  
**Inputs:** Direct chat

```text
Build one complete Meridian strategic record for RelayDock in continuous mode. Cover Levels 0-15 in one run, keep materially different actors separate, and mark assumptions instead of pausing for routine approval. This is an internal working record, so make it concise but complete.

RelayDock is B2B freight-exception software for regional carriers. The carrier dispatcher must recover shipment disruptions before service promises fail. The shipper-support agent must give the customer an accurate promise. A finance reviewer later needs auditable evidence for disputed detention charges.

In May 2024, we interviewed eight dispatchers across two carriers and reviewed 460 exception records. Dispatchers moved among TMS notes, email, calls, and a shared spreadsheet. Teams used status labels differently. Thirty-one percent of sampled records lacked a named next owner; 18% were reopened because a handoff or required document was missed. Shipper-support agents said they often called dispatch for an update before replying to customers. We have no direct finance interviews.

The company chose regional carriers as the first market. The product would integrate with, not replace, the TMS. Our stated advantage was fast onboarding through configurable rules. We explicitly chose not to build a freight marketplace. The experience policy was: preserve one understandable case history, expose owner and next action at every handoff, and make exceptions recoverable without restarting the case.

For the first release, leadership selected late-pickup and missing-document exceptions. Detention disputes and supervisor trend analysis were deferred. We selected a shared exception workspace after comparing it with a notification-only concept and a new standalone operations console. The workspace used an exception case, timeline, owner, next action, evidence request, and resolution as its core objects and states. It needed TMS event ingestion, rule configuration, assignment, evidence upload, notifications, audit history, permissions, and reporting export.

Six moderated workflow tests were run with dispatchers. Five completed recovery without help. One reassignment case failed because ownership history disappeared; the team changed the timeline and repeated that case successfully with the same participant. Engineering implemented the release, QA verified the agreed state transitions, and design reviewed the final interface. It went live for two carrier teams in February 2025.

During the first four live weeks, 182 exceptions went through RelayDock. In an earlier four-week comparison period, the two teams recorded 96 internal status-request emails; in the live period they recorded 61. Median exception-resolution time changed from 11.2 hours to 8.7 hours. The periods were not controlled for shipment mix, staffing, season, or other process changes. No retention, SLA-penalty, customer-promise accuracy, or finance outcome was measured.

Please keep business value connected, but do not let it replace the human outcomes.
```
