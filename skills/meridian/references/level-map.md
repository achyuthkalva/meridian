# The level map keeps one line of meaning from purpose to outcome

This map is Meridian's classification backbone. It preserves the person as reasoning moves from broad purpose to concrete delivery and live learning.

## Experience Ecosystem keeps the surrounding system visible across every level

The [Experience Ecosystem](experience-ecosystem.md) is an orientation and traceability view, not an additional numbered level. Use it when several actors, organizations, channels, policies, technologies, or dependencies shape the experience.

It can inform every level: who is present, how purposes differ, where power sits, what relationships shape the current experience, which dependencies influence strategy, and what system effects should be learned after release. Each material claim still belongs at the numbered level whose question and decision job it answers.

## Sixteen fixed levels reduce abstraction without losing the person

Use these names, numbers, and governing questions exactly. The final column defines the normal classification boundary, not the level guide.

| Level | Fixed name | Governing question | Normal resolution |
| :---| :---| :---| :---|
| 0 | Human Purpose in Context | What purpose is the person pursuing, independent of our product? | Meaningful progress in a real life or work context. |
| 1 | Current Experience | How does this happen today? | Present sequence across people, tools, channels, and conditions. |
| 2 | Problem and Opportunity Space | What evidenced needs, frictions, desires, risks, and leverage points exist? | Structured evidence without selecting a solution. |
| 3 | Strategic UX Outcome | Whose lived experience should improve, and in what observable way? | Chosen human change and indicators, not a feature or business metric alone. |
| 4 | Experience Vision | What could that person’s future lived experience become? | Coherent future-state experience, not the plan for reaching it. |
| 5 | Experience Strategy | What experience choices will move the present toward that vision? | Diagnosis, guiding policy, coherent experience actions, and non-choices. |
| 6 | Product Strategy | Which important problems or bets merit product investment? | Problem bets, advantage, horizon, constraints, and deliberate non-choices. |
| 7 | Experience Roadmap and Priorities | Which actor-specific experience areas deserve attention now, next, and later? | Living strategy artifact with prioritized experience areas, horizons, rationale, dependencies, and non-choices. |
| 8 | Critical Experience | Within a priority, what bounded and consequential experience must work? | Actor, initiating context, meaningful result, qualities, and recovery conditions. |
| 9 | Scenarios and Journeys | For whom, when, under what conditions, and through what sequence does it occur? | Context-rich sequence, needs, stakes, breakdowns, and recovery. |
| 10 | Solution Concepts | What different approaches could work? | Meaningfully different mechanisms, assumptions, tests, and trade-offs. |
| 11 | Enabling Capabilities and Features | What product, service, and technical mechanisms are required? | Stable abilities and justified feature mechanisms. |
| 12 | Interaction Architecture | How should people and the system cooperate across information, actions, states, and rules? | IA, flows, state and permission models, behavior, and interaction rules. |
| 13 | Interface Definition and Prototyping | How is the interaction architecture expressed and explored at interface resolution? | Surfaces, content, states, visual hierarchy, patterns, and testable prototypes. |
| 14 | Validation and Implementation Definition | What does evidence support, and is accepted behavior clear enough to build and verify? | Bounded findings, decisions, specifications, acceptance, and instrumentation. |
| 15 | Delivery, Live Experience, and Outcome Learning | What was released, what happens in operation, and what should the team revisit? | Implementation fidelity, live experience, outcome evidence, and learning decisions. |

Do not add **Product Direction** as a level. Classify valid direction content by its real job:

- experience stance and coherent choices → Level 5;
- product problem bets and investment choices → Level 6;
- actor-specific experience areas and sequencing → Level 7.

## Three reciprocal strategy levels keep human and product choices aligned

Levels 5–7 are ordered for classification, but they interact:

- Experience Strategy shapes what good product investment should enable.
- Product Strategy constrains and funds which experience changes are credible now.
- Experience Roadmap and Priorities integrates both into a living strategy artifact for actor-specific attention over time.

Do not collapse them into one “strategy” statement. Do not force a project-level artifact to prove a company-wide Product Strategy.

## Four boundary tests catch the most common category errors

Ask these questions whenever a claim could fit more than one level:

1. **What object is this claim about?** A person’s purpose, current evidence, desired change, future experience, choice, priority, scenario, mechanism, behavior, interface, or live result?
2. **What decision does it enable?** Understanding, selection, sequencing, concept choice, build definition, release, or learning?
3. **What must still remain open?** If the claim makes a later choice inevitable too early, move or rewrite it.
4. **What sits directly above and below it?** Require one credible upstream justification and one downstream consequence when applicable.

Typical hard boundaries:

- A future experience is Level 4; the choices for reaching it are Level 5.
- An experience policy is Level 5; a product investment bet is Level 6.
- A bet is Level 6; its actor-specific sequencing is Level 7.
- A bounded consequential experience is Level 8; its situated sequence is Level 9.
- A possible approach is Level 10; a stable enabling ability or feature is Level 11.
- System behavior is Level 12; its interface expression is Level 13.
- bounded test and implementation evidence is Level 14; real operation and outcomes are Level 15.

## The final level loops evidence upstream instead of ending at release

At Level 15, separate:

- what was implemented;
- what was released;
- whether implementation matched the accepted definition;
- how the product-service system behaved in real conditions;
- what people experienced;
- what human and organizational changes were observed or measured;
- what contribution or causation the evidence supports; and
- which upstream component must be retained, revised, or reopened.

A shipped feature completes delivery. It does not complete the experience-first chain.
