## Terminology

### Terminology Status

**Playbook-synthesized label; source-adapted concept.**

“Human Purpose in Context” is not presented as a universal industry term. It combines Indi Young’s problem-space emphasis on people pursuing a purpose independently of an offering with GOV.UK’s requirement to understand the person’s full context beyond the government interaction.[source](source-index.md#src-young-problem) [source](source-index.md#src-young-problem-research) [source](source-index.md#src-gov-needs)

## Definition

Human Purpose in Context is an evidence-grounded account of the meaningful progress a person is trying to make in life or work, together with the circumstances, stakes, relationships, constraints, reasoning, and emotions that shape that pursuit. It is framed independently of the organization’s current product, service, channel, or proposed solution.

“Purpose” need not be lofty or permanent. It can be practical and situational, but it must remain more durable and human than a product task such as “submit this form” or “use search.”

**Human purpose does not mean consumer aspiration.** In workplace, enterprise, government, and multi-sided systems, it includes the meaningful progress, responsibility, judgment, or obligation pursued by a person acting in a professional or institutional role.

| Operating context | What Level 0 asks |
| :--- | :--- |
| **B2C** | What meaningful progress is the person trying to make in their life? |
| **B2B** | What meaningful progress is the person trying to make through their professional role? |
| **B2G** | What meaningful progress is the citizen or government worker trying to make within a public-service context? |
| **B2B2C or another multi-sided system** | What distinct purposes are the buyer, administrator, operator, approver, and ultimate beneficiary pursuing? |

Each consequential actor may therefore require a separate Level 0 formulation and experience chain. These chains can converge into one product-service strategy, but they must not be collapsed into one generic “user.” Organizational objectives remain connected to these purposes; they inform strategy, constraints, capabilities, and outcome measurement, but do not substitute for the human purposes themselves.

## Governing question

> **What meaningful progress is this person trying to make in this situation, independent of our product or organization?**

## Strategic job

This level establishes the human territory within which the initiative has meaning. It:

- prevents the current product from defining the problem;
- reveals alternative ways people already pursue the same purpose;
- exposes actors, contexts, and consequences outside the product boundary;
- gives later strategy a durable human anchor; and
- creates a test for whether the organization is improving life or merely increasing product use.

## Unit of analysis and scope

The unit is **one meaningful purpose pursued by a specific behavioral actor in a relevant class of situations**.

The scope should be:

- broader than a product interaction or transactional task;
- narrower than a universal aspiration such as “be happy” or “succeed in life”;
- independent of the organization’s existing solution;
- specific enough to guide research; and
- capable of being pursued through several different products, services, people, or offline approaches.

Different actors may participate in the same ecosystem while pursuing different purposes. Do not collapse a citizen, caseworker, approver, administrator, and regulator into one generic “user purpose.”

## Expert and source anchors

Indi Young explicitly separates problem-space knowledge from an offering and directs attention toward how people reason toward a larger purpose. Her work also stresses inner thinking, emotional reactions, guiding principles, and context-sensitive approaches rather than opinions about a solution.[source](source-index.md#src-young-problem) [source](source-index.md#src-young-listening) [source](source-index.md#src-young-problem-research)

GOV.UK similarly instructs teams to examine the person’s full context and what they are trying to achieve beyond the point where they interact with government.[source](source-index.md#src-gov-needs) [source](source-index.md#src-gov-discovery)

The final label, scope rule, formula, and quality tests below are this playbook’s synthesis.

## Reasoning formula

```text
HUMAN PURPOSE IN CONTEXT
= specific actor or behavioral group
+ situation or trigger
+ meaningful progress sought in life or work
+ human stakes or reason it matters
+ social, environmental, institutional, and personal conditions
+ characteristic reasoning, emotions, or guiding principles where evidenced
− product, feature, interface, channel, or organizational solution
```

The formula is a reasoning aid, not a sentence template. Do not fill a missing element by invention merely to complete the structure.

## What belongs

- The person or behavioral group whose experience matters.
- The situation, trigger, and surrounding conditions.
- The meaningful progress they seek.
- Why that progress matters to them.
- Relevant emotional, social, physical, financial, legal, or professional stakes.
- Reasoning patterns and guiding principles supported by research.
- Constraints such as time, knowledge, ability, authority, risk, access, or dependency.
- Other people and institutions that shape the pursuit.
- Alternative ways the person can pursue the same purpose.
- Meaningful variation between contexts or thinking styles.

## What does not belong

- “Use our portal,” “complete checkout,” or another product task.
- A feature request, interface behavior, or solution concept.
- The organization’s revenue, adoption, efficiency, or compliance objective.
- A broad slogan such as “live a better life.”
- Demographic description without evidence that it changes the purpose or approach.
- A persona biography containing irrelevant lifestyle detail.
- A presumed motivation inferred only from the feature that was ultimately built.
- A desired future experience; that belongs at Levels 3 and 4.

## Adjacent-level boundaries

**Above Level 0:** There is no higher project level in this hierarchy. Societal conditions, ethics, organizational purpose, policy, and market forces may constrain the territory, but they do not replace the person’s purpose.

**Below — Level 1, Current Experience:** Level 0 states what meaningful progress the person is pursuing and why it matters. Level 1 documents how that pursuit currently unfolds in practice.

Level 0 may include durable contextual stakes, reasoning, and constraints. Moment-by-moment actions, reactions, and transitions belong in Level 1.

## Required evidence

Strong evidence may include:

- generative or problem-space interviews grounded in recent, specific experiences;
- contextual inquiry or ethnographic observation;
- diary or longitudinal studies;
- mental-model or cognitive-empathy research;
- accounts from multiple behavioral perspectives;
- artifacts people use while pursuing the purpose; and
- corroborating service, operational, policy, or domain evidence.

Questions about the current product alone are insufficient. Where direct research is unavailable, the limitation must remain visible.

## Permitted reconstruction

**Permitted:**

- synthesize a purpose from repeated motives and stakes visible across contemporaneous project artifacts;
- use confirmed recollection to clarify why an actor was pursuing a workflow;
- use authoritative external research to establish surrounding legal, institutional, or market context; and
- retain several plausible purpose formulations until evidence distinguishes them.

**Not permitted:**

- reverse-translate the shipped feature into the purpose it would conveniently appear to serve;
- claim the team conducted purpose research when it did not;
- invent emotional, social, or motivational detail;
- treat external domain research as evidence of what this project’s actors thought; or
- imply that today’s formulation was explicitly used during the historical project unless documented.

## Claim, lifecycle, and ownership controls

- **Component availability:** Required for any project presented as experience-first. Use **Unknown** or **Partially evidenced** rather than fabricate it. “Not applicable” requires exceptional justification.
- **Provenance:** Classify each purpose claim as D, R, C, I, or U. External context is C unless project evidence connects it to the historical work.
- **Lifecycle:** A purpose is not “released” or “implemented.” Use component availability and provenance to show whether the formulation is evidenced, partial, reconstructed, or unknown; use lifecycle states only for downstream interventions and outcome claims.
- **Ownership:** Use “I researched,” “I synthesized,” or “I helped frame” only when supported. Do not convert team research into sole personal discovery.

## Writing grammar

**Formula**

> **When [specific actor] is [situation], they are trying to [meaningful progress in life or work] because [human stakes], while navigating [relevant conditions or constraints].**

**Illustrative application**

> When a listener begins a workout after a demanding day, they are trying to shift their energy and attention so they can enter the activity, while managing fatigue and limited attention for choosing music.

The product is intentionally absent. “Open Spotify and find a workout playlist” would be a current product task, not the human purpose.

## Canonical output and traceability

The canonical output is a **Human Purpose in Context frame** containing:

- actor or behavioral group;
- situation and trigger;
- purpose statement;
- human stakes;
- contextual conditions and constraints;
- evidenced variations or thinking styles;
- evidence references and provenance;
- uncertainties and alternative formulations; and
- scope boundary.

It has no internal upstream parent. It must justify the scope of the Level 1 current-experience model and remain traceable through the Strategic UX Outcome.

## Quality audit

**Pass when:**

- the purpose could still exist if the current product disappeared;
- several different approaches could help the person pursue it;
- the actor, situation, progress, and stakes are understandable;
- product language has not contaminated the statement; and
- evidence and uncertainty are explicit.

**Fail when:**

- the statement is merely a task, feature, channel, or business objective;
- the scope is so broad that it cannot guide inquiry;
- an internal role label substitutes for a human actor without context; or
- the final solution is the only evidence for the alleged purpose.

**Experience-first leadership signal:** The designer starts above the product, protects the person’s agency and context, and gives the team a durable human reason for the work.
