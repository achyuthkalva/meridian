# Experience Convergence — Watch One Human Need Become a Live Learning Loop

This compact example shows how a single idea becomes more concrete across Meridian without losing the original person, context, or intended improvement. It is a teaching aid, not project evidence or a mandatory stage-gate process.

## One idea changes resolution at every level

The example below makes the hierarchy visible by showing how its language changes as a team moves toward a concrete, testable experience.

```text
Human Purpose
“I use music to shape how I feel and what I am doing.”

        ↓

Current Experience
“I often know the kind of feeling I want, but not the song or artist.”

        ↓

Opportunity
“Choosing requires too much browsing when intent is vague.”

        ↓

UX Outcome
“People reach satisfying audio with less effort and uncertainty.”

        ↓

Experience Vision
“Music adapts naturally to the person and the moment.”

        ↓

Experience Strategy
“Move from catalogue navigation toward intent-aware guidance.”

        ↓

Product Strategy
“Prioritize vague-intent discovery as a high-value problem.”

        ↓

Experience Roadmap and Priorities
“Prioritize vague-intent discovery now; sequence cross-context continuity and deeper steering next.”

        ↓

Critical Experience
“Find something that fits this moment without knowing what to search for.”

        ↓

Scenario
“After work, I begin a 45-minute workout and want energetic music immediately.”

        ↓

Solution Concepts
“Ask by voice / choose a context / infer context / start a personalized session.”

        ↓

Enabling Capabilities and Features
“Intent input + recommendation service + taste model + playback + feedback.”

        ↓

Interaction Architecture
“Express intent → receive session → lightly steer → continue listening.”

        ↓

Interface Definition and Prototyping
“Home entry point → intent input → generated session → steering states.”

        ↓

Validation and Implementation Definition
“Test the intended-use claims → record bounded evidence → define build and verification requirements.”

        ↓

Delivery, Live Experience, and Outcome Learning
“Verify the released configuration → observe the live experience → measure bounded outcomes → revise upstream decisions.”
```

At no point is the screen disconnected from the original human purpose. That traceability is one of Meridian’s primary benefits: it lets the team test whether a concrete decision still serves the change the person needs.

## The example ends by returning the decision to the person

Use this guide when a team needs to show how strategy, prioritization, behavior, interface, and live evidence remain part of one accountable line.
