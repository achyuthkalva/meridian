# The interaction model finds the smallest honest path

## Seven controls locate the work before the process begins

Do not choose a workflow from the volume or polish of the input. Locate the request across seven independent controls.

| Control | Options | Why it matters |
| :---| :---| :---|
| Intent | Orient, normalize, construct, reconstruct, audit, research, reconcile, compare, facilitate, evaluate, translate | Selects the kind of reasoning required. |
| Scope | One level, boundary pair, module, full chain, ecosystem, portfolio | Sets the resolution and prevents unwanted expansion. |
| Time | Present/future, historical, mixed | Changes the evidence and claim rules. |
| Actors | One actor, linked actors, ecosystem | Prevents a generic “user” from hiding different purposes or risks. |
| Evidence | Complete, partial, messy, conflicting, absent | Sets how far the agent may proceed. |
| Cadence | Guided, controlled, continuous, rapid triage, review-only | Defines where the agent stops. |
| Output | Working record, decision aid, strategic artifact, delivery artifact, public narrative | Sets structure and publication risk. |

Use this compact working-contract form when it helps:

> **Intent:** [mode] · **Scope:** [resolution] · **Time:** [orientation] · **Actors:** [topology] · **Evidence:** [condition] · **Cadence:** [rhythm] · **Output:** [artifact]

Infer obvious controls. Ask only about a control whose alternatives would materially change truth, scope, commitment, safety, or the output.

## Eleven intent modes turn different starting points into useful work

Modes describe what the user needs now. They do not change the locked strategic levels.

| Mode | Use when | First useful result |
| :---| :---| :---|
| Orient and locate | The user is unsure where an idea, artifact, or problem belongs. | Level and boundary diagnosis. |
| Intake and normalize | Inputs arrive in mixed, messy, or inconsistent forms. | Source inventory and normalized evidence ledger. |
| Construct | A current or future initiative needs part or all of the chain. | Candidate components with traceability. |
| Reconstruct | Historical reasoning is incomplete or poorly documented. | Evidence-controlled historical chain with explicit gaps. |
| Audit and repair | Existing strategy, case study, roadmap, or design logic may be misclassified or weak. | Findings, hard failures, and repairs. |
| Research and contextualize | Domain, market, policy, technology, or category context is missing. | Sourced context separated from team history. |
| Reconcile and update | New evidence or recollection may change an existing chain. | Change map, retained claims, revised claims, and superseded claims. |
| Compare and decide | Several priorities, strategies, concepts, or claims compete. | Criteria, trade-offs, evidence, and a human decision point. |
| Facilitate and align | A group needs shared language or a structured decision. | Agenda, prompts, decision record, and unresolved tensions. |
| Evaluate and learn | Validation or live evidence must be interpreted. | Bounded findings, outcome status, and upstream changes. |
| Translate and communicate | An approved chain must become a case study, brief, roadmap, interview answer, or delivery record. | Audience-fit narrative without claim inflation. |

Combine modes only when the sequence is necessary. For example, historical work may use **Intake → Reconstruct → Audit → Translate**. State the current mode so the user knows what is—and is not—being approved.

Do not create separate modes for conditions already covered by another control:

- “one level” and “full process” are scopes;
- “B2B” and “multi-sided” are actor contexts;
- “guided” and “background batch” are cadences;
- “PDF” and “connector” are input routes;
- “red team” is a strict audit stance;
- “case study” is an output form.

## Scope expands only as far as the user’s decision requires

### A single level still receives boundary checks

When the user asks for one level, formulate or audit that level. Inspect only enough of the level above and below to prevent classification error and preserve traceability.

### A boundary pair resolves common category confusion

Use a two-level comparison when content is drifting across adjacent levels, such as:

- Experience Vision vs Experience Strategy;
- Experience Strategy vs Product Strategy;
- Product Strategy vs Experience Roadmap and Priorities;
- Critical Experience vs Scenario and Journey;
- Solution Concept vs Capability and Feature;
- Interaction Architecture vs Interface Definition;
- Validation evidence vs live outcome evidence.

### A module creates a reviewable unit without breaking the chain

Recommended working modules are:

1. **Foundation:** Levels 0–3.
2. **Direction and choices:** Levels 4–7.
3. **Experience and possibilities:** Levels 8–10.
4. **System and expression:** Levels 11–13.
5. **Evidence, delivery, and learning:** Levels 14–15.

These are collaboration units, not new levels. Always preserve links across module boundaries.

### A full chain can still begin where the evidence begins

Do not force a top-down workshop when the user begins with screens, requirements, analytics, or a released product. Classify what exists, move upward and downward to test its meaning, and mark unsupported gaps as Unknown.

### Multi-actor and portfolio scopes need separate lines of meaning

Create linked actor chains when different people pursue different purposes or carry different risks. At portfolio scope, compare experience areas and sequencing without pretending that each project has a complete company strategy.

## Different evidence conditions change the route, not the standard

### Complete information moves quickly to verification

Confirm the working contract, check source authority and chronology, run classification and traceability tests, then produce the requested artifact. Do not repeat discovery questions already answered by the evidence.

### Incomplete information moves until the next material gap

Proceed with supported claims. Label partial components and working inferences. Ask for the smallest piece of information that would unlock the next consequential decision.

### Messy information is normalized before it is interpreted

Preserve originals. Create stable source IDs. Separate raw statements, observations, decisions, later summaries, and agent interpretations before building a polished chain.

### Conflicting information becomes a decision record

Do not silently choose the most polished or recent claim. Compare authority, date, proximity to the event, independence, and fit with other evidence. Resolve reversible discrepancies provisionally. Pause when equally credible alternatives would change public truth or strategic commitment.

### Missing information produces honest options, not invented history

When no legitimate project evidence exists, provide one or more of:

- an Unknown classification;
- explicit hypotheses for future research;
- a question set;
- a context-only research brief; or
- a template that remains visibly unfilled.

Do not call a plausible story a reconstruction when no project evidence anchors it.

## Time orientation decides whether the agent may create or only recover

### Present and future work can generate new strategic choices

Use evidence, facilitated judgment, and research to construct candidate components. Mark hypotheses and obtain human commitment at consequential strategy and concept gates.

### Historical work can recover meaning but cannot rewrite chronology

Separate:

- what was known or decided at the time;
- what downstream artifacts imply when corroborated;
- what the user now remembers and confirms;
- what external sources establish about the surrounding context; and
- what Meridian proposes today as a clearer interpretation.

Never imply that current terminology, Meridian, or the full chain was used historically unless contemporaneous evidence supports it.

### Mixed work keeps past truth separate from present recommendation

Use explicit sections such as **What the evidence supports about the original work** and **What we would strengthen now**. Do not merge them into one seamless narrative.

## Cadence makes the user’s involvement predictable

| Cadence | Agent behavior | User involvement |
| :---| :---| :---|
| Guided | Work one meaningful decision at a time. | Respond at each decision gate. |
| Controlled | Complete and verify one approved module, then stop. | Review each module; answer bundled questions; approve or revise. |
| Continuous | Complete all reversible modules in one run. | Intervene only at critical gates or final review. |
| Rapid triage | Find the highest-risk gaps and claims first. | Confirm priorities or choose the next deep dive. |
| Review-only | Diagnose without rewriting unless asked. | Decide whether repairs may begin. |

Default rules:

- Use controlled cadence for historical reconstruction, sensitive work, high-stakes claims, and durable public artifacts.
- Use continuous cadence when the user explicitly asks for a complete run and no critical human gate is pending.
- Continue within an approved module without requesting routine approvals.
- Bundle non-blocking questions at the checkpoint.
- Stop immediately for a blocker that changes truth, authority, privacy, or irreversible commitment.

At each controlled checkpoint, report:

1. what is complete;
2. what changed;
3. what remains open;
4. what evidence or decision is needed;
5. how many approvals remain under the current plan; and
6. what the next module will do.

## Human gates reserve judgment where automation would overreach

| Gate | Agent may prepare | Human must decide |
| :---| :---| :---|
| Actor and scope | Map plausible actors, boundaries, and consequences. | Select a materially consequential framing. |
| Historical truth | Compare sources and draft calibrated alternatives. | Confirm disputed intent, chronology, or recollection. |
| Ownership | Trace artifacts, roles, and decision records. | Confirm ambiguous personal or team contribution. |
| Strategic commitment | Diagnose, model choices, and show trade-offs. | Adopt the vision, policy, bet, priority, or non-choice. |
| Evidence conflict | Rank sources and explain implications. | Resolve equally credible claims with different consequences. |
| Concept commitment | Generate and evaluate meaningfully different concepts. | Select or authorize a concept direction. |
| Outcome claim | Analyze signals and alternative explanations. | Approve contribution or causal wording when judgment is required. |
| Publication and privacy | Flag sensitive data and claim risk. | Authorize disclosure, redaction policy, or release. |
| Approval and lock | Run audits and list unresolved issues. | Approve, lock, supersede, or reopen the artifact. |

Record every critical decision with its owner, date or period when known, rationale, affected claims, and reopening condition.

## Resumption begins from the checkpoint instead of restarting the process

When work resumes after a pause:

1. restate the last approved module and version;
2. list decisions that are locked, provisional, or reopened;
3. ingest only new or changed evidence;
4. show which claims the new evidence affects;
5. continue from the next approved module.

Do not redo completed work unless new evidence, a contradiction, or a human decision invalidates it.
