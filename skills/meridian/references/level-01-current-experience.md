## Terminology

### Terminology Status

**Common UX language; source-adapted scope.**

“Current experience” and “current-state journey” are widely used rather than owned by one framework. This playbook defines the level as the evidenced, end-to-end reality of pursuing Level 0 across product and non-product touchpoints.

## Definition

Current Experience is an evidence-grounded model of what people actually do, think, feel, decide, coordinate, and encounter today while pursuing the Level 0 purpose—before, during, between, and after the organization’s touchpoints.

It includes official processes and lived behavior. Where those differ, both must be shown.

## Governing question

> **How is this purpose pursued today across people, tools, channels, institutions, decisions, and workarounds?**

## Strategic job

This level replaces organizational assumptions with a shared account of present reality. It:

- exposes the full journey rather than only the owned interface;
- reveals dependencies, channel switching, hidden labor, and workarounds;
- distinguishes actual behavior from prescribed process;
- gives Level 2 an evidence base for interpretation; and
- creates a baseline against which future experience change can be evaluated.

## Unit of analysis and scope

The unit is **one actor’s evidence-backed pursuit of the Level 0 purpose across a defined start and end boundary**.

The model may contain multiple pathways when context, authority, ability, or actor type materially changes the experience. It should include:

- events before the organization first becomes visible;
- relevant interactions with the product or service;
- handoffs to other people, systems, or institutions;
- offline and support channels;
- waiting, interruption, recovery, and abandonment; and
- consequences after the nominal transaction ends.

Do not force every actor into one average journey.

## Expert and source anchors

GOV.UK discovery guidance asks teams to learn who people are, what they are trying to do, how they currently do it, which channels they use, and which barriers they experience. It also explicitly includes support staff, offline steps, workflow data, and existing research.[source](source-index.md#src-gov-research-discovery)

Jared Spool’s journey-to-vision method begins by observing people using current solutions—including other tools and processes—and then looks for patterns across individual journeys. His strategic-UX work treats deep awareness of the current experience as a prerequisite for better decisions.[source](source-index.md#src-spool-journey) [source](source-index.md#src-spool-current-awareness)

Chris Risdon’s experience-map anatomy adds cross-channel touchpoints, doing–thinking–feeling, qualitative and quantitative evidence, and purposeful scope.[source](source-index.md#src-risdon-experience-map)

## Reasoning formula

```text
CURRENT EXPERIENCE
= Level 0 actor and purpose
+ trigger and start condition
+ actual sequence of actions, decisions, interactions, and waiting
+ people, tools, channels, systems, policies, and information involved
+ thoughts, expectations, emotions, and guiding principles where evidenced
+ constraints, dependencies, workarounds, failures, and recovery
+ present outcomes and consequences
+ variation, frequency, and evidence confidence
```

## What belongs

- Observable behavior and recent-event accounts.
- Before, during, between, and after moments.
- Product, service, human, institutional, and offline touchpoints.
- Information sought, created, transferred, re-entered, or lost.
- Decisions and dependencies.
- Waiting, interruption, channel switching, and coordination burden.
- Workarounds, shadow tools, assistance, and abandonment.
- Thoughts and emotions supported by research.
- Accessibility, environmental, authority, and capability constraints.
- Positive moments and successful existing practices—not only pain.
- Meaningful variants and contradictory evidence.

## What does not belong

- The intended future journey.
- A redesigned workflow or proposed feature.
- A root-cause conclusion presented as observation.
- A prioritized opportunity or strategic choice.
- The documented standard operating procedure presented as actual behavior without verification.
- A screen flow that begins and ends at the product boundary.
- Unsupported emotion inferred from interface appearance.
- An idealized “happy path” that removes waiting, failure, or human coordination.

## Adjacent-level boundaries

**Above — Level 0, Human Purpose in Context:** Level 0 establishes what progress matters and why. Level 1 shows how people currently attempt to make that progress.

Level 0 contains the durable purpose, stakes, and contextual frame. Level 1 contains the moment-by-moment enactment and experience of pursuing it.

**Below — Level 2, Problem and Opportunity Space:** Level 1 is primarily descriptive: what happens and how it is experienced. Level 2 is interpretive: which needs, frictions, desires, risks, causes, and leverage points the evidence suggests.

## Required evidence

Use a purposeful combination of:

- contextual observation using real environments, documents, devices, and distractions;
- interviews anchored in recent, specific events;
- journey walkthroughs and artifact elicitation;
- analytics, event logs, search logs, and funnel data;
- support contacts, complaints, training records, and operational logs;
- caseworker or service-provider observation;
- workflow, policy, and process documentation; and
- prior research with known date, scope, and participant coverage.

Contextual observation is especially valuable for seeing barriers and workarounds that participants or internal teams may omit from a verbal account.[source](source-index.md#src-gov-contextual)

## Permitted reconstruction

**Permitted:**

- synthesize a current-state sequence from multiple contemporaneous artifacts;
- reconstruct a missing transition when adjacent evidence and confirmed recollection converge;
- use released software and dated workflow evidence to confirm available states;
- mark an official process separately from a hypothesized lived path; and
- use external sources to explain public rules or ecosystem structure.

**Not permitted:**

- infer thoughts or emotions from a workflow diagram;
- convert an intended requirement into proof of actual behavior;
- claim prevalence from one anecdote;
- treat today’s released product as proof of the experience at an earlier project date; or
- smooth gaps into an apparently complete journey without identifying uncertainty.

## Claim, lifecycle, and ownership controls

- **Component availability:** Required. If evidence covers only the owned product, classify the component as **Partially evidenced**, not complete.
- **Provenance:** Tag each important step or insight D, R, C, I, or U where evidence strength varies.
- **Time boundary:** State the period represented. “Current” is not timeless; processes, policies, and products change.
- **Lifecycle:** Distinguish an observed current experience from a designed or released replacement experience.
- **Ownership:** Prefer “I mapped,” “I synthesized,” “I observed,” or “we researched” according to the evidence and team roles.

## Writing grammar

**Formula**

> **Today, when [trigger], [actor] pursues [Level 0 purpose] by [evidence-backed sequence] across [people, tools, and channels]. They encounter [observed conditions or workarounds], producing [present human consequence].**

**Illustrative application**

> Today, when a listener wants energy for a workout but has no song in mind, they scan recent items, browse recommendations, try vague searches, sample tracks, and skip between playlists. The active choosing continues until something feels suitable, consuming attention they wanted to return to the workout.

## Canonical output and traceability

The canonical output is a **Current Experience Model**, normally a journey, ecosystem map, or evidence-linked narrative containing:

- actor, purpose, scope, start, and end;
- phases, actions, decisions, and transitions;
- people, systems, channels, objects, and policies;
- doing, thinking, and feeling where evidenced;
- dependencies, waiting, failure, recovery, and workaround behavior;
- present outcomes and consequences;
- variants, prevalence where known, and counterevidence;
- source references, dates, and provenance; and
- open questions.

It must trace upward to the Level 0 purpose and downward to specific Level 2 opportunity nodes. A map without evidence references is a communication artifact, not sufficient proof.

## Quality audit

**Pass when:**

- the journey extends beyond the owned interface where reality does;
- observed behavior is distinguishable from interpretation;
- official process and lived behavior are not conflated;
- start, end, actors, variants, and evidence are visible; and
- successful moments, failures, and workarounds are represented proportionately.

**Fail when:**

- the artifact is merely a screen inventory or ideal happy path;
- steps, emotions, or prevalence were invented to make the story coherent;
- the time period is unclear; or
- it cannot show which evidence supports consequential claims.

**Experience-first leadership signal:** The designer creates shared, cross-functional exposure to people’s real experience and expands the team’s attention beyond its product and organizational boundaries.
